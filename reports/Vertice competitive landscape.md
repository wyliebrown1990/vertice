# Vertice Wins Its Niche, Then Meets Giants

*Competitive landscape and exit-odds brief for Vertice (vertice.one). Data as of 15 October 2026.*

## Executive brief

**Bottom line:** Vertice has become the clear leader of the narrow "SaaS buying and negotiation" niche. Its 1 June 2026 purchase of Vendr removed its largest rival and left one scaled independent head-to-head competitor, Tropic. The fight has since moved to broader procurement and spend platforms, where Vertice is smaller and has raised less money than Zip, Ramp and Coupa. On the model in this brief, Vertice has **about a 27% chance (range 18–38%) of a successful exit by October 2031**. That is roughly 1.5 times the base rate for a comparable Series B/C company. It has **about an 11% chance (range 6–24%) of exiting at or above its reported ~$500M Series C valuation**. The single most likely outcome is that it is **still private in five years (~46%)**, probably after raising again. The most likely exit route is a **strategic sale to a US platform or a PE-backed procurement suite**. An IPO is a rounding error within this horizon.

| Headline odds, 5-year horizon (to Oct 2031) | Low | **Base** | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Successful exit (IPO + strategic sale ≥$500M + PE buyout + sale at $250–500M) | 18% | **27%** | 38% |
| Exit at or above the ~$500M last-round valuation | 6% | **11%** | 24% |
| Shutdown or fire sale | 28% | **14%** | 8% |
| Still private at 5 years | 44% | **46%** | 43% |

**Key takeaways**

1. **Vertice owns its niche but not its market.** After buying Vendr, Vertice claims more than 1,000 customers and the largest negotiated-price dataset in the category: $75B+ of spend, 250,000 contracts and 2M+ price points. Its next-closest pure-play rival, Tropic, has raised about $67M and has not announced a round since 2022.
2. **The real competitors are far better funded.** Zip has raised $371M at a $2.2B valuation and has an estimated $193–280M in ARR. Ramp announced a $60B valuation today with more than $2B in annualized revenue, and it now ships procurement AI agents. Coupa, owned by Thoma Bravo, bought four AI procurement assets in 12 months, Tonkean among them.
3. **The category is consolidating quickly and mostly at undisclosed prices.** At least 17 transactions have been announced since January 2025. Only one, Capital One–Brex, disclosed a price, at about 42% of Brex's 2021 peak. Productiv was liquidated in mid-2026.
4. **The $500M mark relies on a premium multiple.** At the 2Q26 private SaaS median of 4.0x, $500M requires about $125M of ARR. Vertice's combined ARR is undisclosed. An analyst estimate of $40–80M implies $160–320M at the median multiple and $400–800M at the roughly 10x paid in procurement control deals.
5. **Vertice has real advantages.** Its founders have two prior strategic exits (ScanSafe to Cisco, Wandera to Jamf). It ranked #1 on Sifted's 2025 UK growth list with a 689% two-year CAGR. Its disclosed preference stack is small, about $101M against roughly $500M. It was named an IDC MarketScape Leader in 2026.
6. **Its weaknesses are specific.** Revenue is undisclosed. It has not raised since January 2025 (21 months). It is integrating a shrinking Vendr. It faces commoditisation from Ramp's interchange-subsidised bundle and from free benchmark tiers offered by rivals.
7. **Watch these signals:** a Series D or debt raise; Vertice Group Ltd's UK accounts, due by 31 December 2026; any Vendr brand sunset or staff attrition; how Ramp prices procurement; and Coupa, Workday or ServiceNow M&A.

## Vertice: a fast-growing consolidator with an undisclosed top line

Vertice was incorporated in London on **30 November 2021** and operating from January 2022. It is run by co-CEOs and brothers **Eldar and Roy Tuvey**. They previously sold ScanSafe to Cisco (2009) and Wandera to Jamf (2021) ([Wikipedia: ScanSafe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ScanSafe); [UKTN](https://uktechnews.co.uk/2022/05/20/serial-founders-raise-26m-series-a-for-new-b2b-saas-purchasing-platform/)). Coverage puts those exits at roughly $200M and $400M ([TechRound](https://techround.co.uk/saas-66/8-vertice/)).

Vertice has raised **about $101M across three priced rounds**: a $26M Series A (May 2022, led by 83North and Bessemer), a $25M Series B (January 2024) and a **$50M Series C led by Lakestar in January 2025**. TechCrunch's sources put the Series C valuation at **~$500M** ([TechCrunch, Jan 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/21/vertice-raises-50m-for-its-ai-powered-saas-spend-platform/)).

Growth signals are strong but only relative:

- ARR was in the "double-digit millions" in January 2024, after growing 7x in 2023 ([TechCrunch, Jan 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/17/vertice-raises-25m-for-ai-based-tools-to-help-companies-tackle-software-spend/)).
- Revenue grew 13x over the two years to January 2025 ([Yahoo Finance/PR](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/vertice-raises-50-million-series-084000144.html)).
- Vertice ranked **#1 on Sifted's 2025 UK & Ireland 100** with a **689% two-year revenue CAGR** ([Sifted](https://sifted.eu/articles/spend-platform-vertice-tops-sifteds-second-uk-and-ireland-leaderboard)).

The product has widened from SaaS negotiation into cloud cost optimisation, intake-to-procure workflows, embedded third-party risk management (TPRM) and **Ana**, an autonomous negotiation agent. Vertice reports that Ana has run 4,000+ negotiations covering about $500M of spend, with 18% average savings. These are self-reported figures ([Procurement Magazine](https://procurementmag.com/news/vertice-launches-ana-ai-negotiation-agent-for-software)).

The Vendr deal added the former $1B unicorn's data and its "entire team", which Crustdata estimates at about 199 people ([Vertice blog](https://www.vertice.one/blog/vertice-acquires-vendr); [Crustdata](https://profiles.crustdata.com/company/vendr)). After the deal Vertice claims **1,000+ customers and 60+ AI agents** ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/vertice-acquires-vendr-to-create-the-worlds-largest-procurement-intelligence-dataset-and-lead-autonomous-ai-negotiation-302786407.html)). Analysts recognise it as a **Leader in the IDC MarketScape for AI-Enabled Spend Orchestration 2026** ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/vertice-named-a-leader-in-idc-marketscape-worldwide-ai-enabled-spend-orchestration-2026-vendor-assessment-302836810.html)).

## The odds model puts a successful exit near one in four

The model has four steps. It starts from base rates for a generic Series B/C company, converts them to a five-year horizon, applies eight Vertice-specific adjustment factors as multipliers, and renormalises the result to 100%. Every number below can be recomputed from the tables.

The **outcome priors are analyst estimates**. The researchers found no published Series B/C cohort outcome table, so the priors are synthesised from the proxy data cited in Step 1.

### Step 1: Base rates from cohort data

The best available proxy is Carta's 2018 US founding cohort. Of **4,369 startups**, 643 had reached Series B after about seven years, **15 had IPO'd and 143 had been acquired** ([Carta](https://carta.com/data/linkedin-2018-startup-cohort-outcomes-2025/)). That implies roughly a **2–3% IPO rate conditional on reaching Series B** over that period.

Four other data points shape the priors:

- Carta's 2026 analysis of 3,903 acquired startups found **80% had raised under $50M** ([Founded CEO on Carta](https://foundedceo.substack.com/p/carta-just-released-the-most-honest)). Vertice, at about $101M raised, sits in the thinner tail where buyers are larger.
- Series B shutdowns on Carta rose **133% year on year in Q1 2024** ([Carta](https://carta.com/data/startup-shutdowns-q1-2024/)).
- **38.3% of US unicorns** have not raised in three or more years as of 2026 ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/pro/all-deals/2026/03/27/zombie-unicorn-count-grows)).
- The median gap between Series B and Series C was **2.4 years** at the end of 2024 ([Carta](https://carta.com/data/state-of-private-markets-q4-2024/)).

From these, the exit-rates researcher synthesised ten-year priors for a Series B/C SaaS company. The table takes the midpoints, assumes that about 60% of M&A and shutdown events (and 33% of IPOs) happen within the first five years, and splits the result into the six outcome buckets requested.

| Outcome | 10-yr prior range (analyst) | Midpoint | Share realised by year 5 | Generic 5-yr prior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. IPO | 3–6% | 4.5% | 33% | 1.5% |
| B. Strategic sale ≥ last-round value (~$500M) | 15–25% "good" M&A | 20% | 60% → 12%, split 50/50 between B and C1 | 6.0% |
| C1. Sale below last round but ≥ ~$250M (common still in the money) | (from above) | — | — | 6.0% |
| C2. Preference-protected sale (< ~$250M) | 15–25% below-preference M&A | 20% | 60% → 12%, split 50/50 between C2 and F | 6.0% |
| D. PE buyout | 5–10% | 7.5% | 60% | 4.5% |
| F. Shutdown / fire sale / acqui-hire | 15–25% shutdown + half of below-preference M&A | 20% | 60% | 18.0% |
| E. Still private at 5 years | remainder | — | — | 58.0% |

On this generic prior, the chance of a **successful exit (A + B + C1 + D) is 18%** over five years.

### Step 3: Outcome distribution, low / base / high

The base case uses the factors above. The **low** case assumes weaker growth (factor 2 at 1.0, 1.15 and 0.9), harsher Ramp and Zip pressure (factor 5 at 0.5, 0.65 and 1.4), deeper multiple compression (factor 6 at 0.5 and 0.6 for IPO and ≥$500M) and a troubled Vendr integration (factor 3 at 1.1, 1.1 and 1.25). The **high** case assumes stronger growth (factor 2 at 2.0, 1.6 and 0.6), a smooth integration (factor 3 at 1.5 for ≥$500M), milder competition (factor 5 at 0.8, 0.9 and 1.1) and partial multiple recovery (factor 6 at 0.9 and 0.9). The result: in the base case Vertice is **about 1.5 times more likely than a generic Series B/C company to achieve a successful exit** within five years, at 27% versus 18%. Almost all of that uplift comes from strategic M&A, not from an IPO.

The scenarios differ mainly in **how a sale happens**, not in **whether** one happens. The chance of some liquidity event is stable at 54–57% across all three cases. What moves is the price. In the low case, fire sales (28%) outnumber successful exits (18%). In the high case, a sale at or above $500M becomes the most likely exit outcome.

Two cautions. The C1/C2 split is symmetric by assumption. The ~$250M success line assumes a 1x non-participating preference stack of about $101M. If the Vendr deal created new senior preferred stock, that line rises.

### Step 4: Implied-valuation sanity check

Vertice's combined 2026 ARR is undisclosed. The working range of **$40–80M is an analyst inference**. It comes from 2024 revenue of about $12–25M, implied by "double-digit millions" ARR in January 2024 and 13x two-year growth, plus Vendr's book. Applying observed 2026 multiples:

| Combined ARR | 3.2x (public SaaS median, 2Q26) | 4.0x (private SaaS M&A median, 2Q26) | 6.2x (private average) | 8.5x (Coupa NTM control multiple) | 10x (Jaggaer / Coupa trailing) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $40M | $128M | $160M | $248M | $340M | $400M |
| $60M | $192M | $240M | $372M | $510M | $600M |
| $80M | $256M | $320M | $496M | $680M | $800M |
| **ARR needed for $500M** | $156M | **$125M** | $81M | $59M | $50M |

Multiple sources: [SEG](https://softwareequity.com/research/quarterly-saas-report); [Peony summary of SEG](https://www.peony.ink/blog/saas-valuation-multiples); [diginomica on Coupa NTM](https://diginomica.com/coupa-private-thoma-bravo-8-billion-buyout).

At median 2026 private multiples, Vertice's likely enterprise value is **$160–320M**, below the ~$500M Series C mark. The Series C itself implied about **20–30x trailing revenue** (analyst inference). The mark holds up only if Vertice is valued like a strategic asset at 8–10x, which Coupa, Jaggaer and Brex achieved at scale, or if it **grows into the mark**.

At 40% growth from $60M of ARR, Vertice reaches the $125M needed at a 4x multiple in about **2.2 years (late 2028)**; at 30% growth it takes about 2.8 years. The ~$101M preference stack is about 20% of the $500M mark. Any sale above roughly $150–250M therefore returns all investor capital and leaves meaningful value for common shareholders. That is why bucket C1 counts as a success.

The IPO bar is now about **$300–500M ARR with 30–50% growth** ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/the-2025-ipo-class-graded-only-3-of-13-are-above-water/)). Reaching even $300M from $60M at 40% growth takes about 4.8 years, which puts a listing at the edge of the five-year window at best.

The structure is **fragmented but consolidating quickly**. The SaaS management layer has gone from about nine scaled independents to two or three, Zylo and Lumos among them. The SaaS-buying layer has gone from three funded leaders (Vendr, Vertice, Sastrify) to Vertice plus Tropic. The orchestration layer is the one still fragmenting, with new money arriving. The likely end state is a few platforms plus a handful of data-rich specialists, and Vertice is positioning to be one of those specialists.

### Moats and vulnerabilities

**Moats.** Vertice's most defensible asset is its **negotiated-contract dataset**. The combined Vertice–Vendr dataset covers $75B+ of spend, 250,000 contracts and 2M+ price points. On disclosed claims that is about **3x Tropic's $23B under management** and over 10x Spendflo, Sastrify or CloudEagle. It is also a different kind of data from Ramp's card transactions ([Vendr blog](https://www.vendr.com/blog/vendr-is-joining-vertice)). The hybrid of software and managed negotiation, backed by a savings guarantee, ties revenue to measurable ROI, which matters to CFOs in a cost-cutting market. The other assets are:

- analyst momentum (IDC Leader; Forrester landscape inclusion);
- a named enterprise customer base (ARM, ASML, Twilio, Santander);
- transatlantic reach after Vendr;
- founders who have sold to strategic buyers twice.

**Vulnerabilities.**

- **Capital.** Vertice has raised a fraction of what Zip and Ramp have, and it has not raised in 21 months, nearly the 2.4-year median gap between rounds, while digesting an acquisition.
- **Commoditisation.** Its core benchmark-and-negotiate service is the function Ramp now gives away inside a card platform.
- **Services margins.** Managed negotiation probably carries lower gross margins than pure software. This is an inference, since no margin data exists, but it would depress multiples.
- **Integration.** Vendr shrank roughly 24% in the year to November 2025 ([Getlatka](https://getlatka.com/companies/vendr)).
- **Culture.** Glassdoor reviews describe a "revolving door" in sales ([Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.co.uk/Reviews/Vertice-people-Reviews-EI_IE7573167.0,7_KH8,14.htm)).
- **Transparency.** No revenue has been disclosed. Vertice Group Ltd's accounts are due by 31 December 2026 and will be the first hard data point ([Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/13771845)).

### Key risks and leading indicators

The biggest risk is a **valuation squeeze**. Ramp and the free tiers cap what customers will pay for benchmarks. Public multiples sit at about 3x. Vertice's undisclosed ARR may not justify its 2025 mark. Together these could push the company toward a C2-type preference-protected sale, which is the path Vendr itself followed. The second risk is **capital timing**. Raising a Series D in a market where 38% of unicorns are zombies, without public revenue disclosure, risks a flat or down round. That would reset the success threshold.

The upside case rests on Ana becoming a real agentic product with software-like margins, and on the Vendr data making Vertice the default pricing layer that suites and fintechs license or buy.

## Conclusion

Vertice won a contest that is shrinking in importance. Buying Vendr made it the scale player in negotiated SaaS pricing in the same year that pricing data started to become a free feature: Ramp bundles it with corporate cards, Tropic and Zip expose it through LLM connectors, and Coupa is assembling its own version through acquisitions. Vertice's exit therefore depends less on beating Tropic than on persuading a platform buyer that its dataset and negotiation engine are worth buying rather than building. The 2025–26 deal record shows platforms buy aggressively, but usually at undisclosed and probably modest prices.

The model's most useful output is that Vertice is more likely than average to exit, at about 54% versus 42% for a generic Series B/C company, and that its outcome turns on price rather than survival. On this evidence, a sale in the $250–500M range is more likely than one above $500M. The first hard numbers, from the December 2026 Companies House filing and any Series D, will show which side of $500M Vertice is on.
