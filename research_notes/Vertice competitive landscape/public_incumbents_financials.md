## Key Question 1: What are the revenue scale and growth of the incumbents (actual reported numbers, with fiscal years)?

### Takeaway
The public "bounding" platforms are large but grow slowly: SAP at €36.8B (FY2025, +8%), ServiceNow at $13.3B (FY2025, +21%) and Workday at $9.55B (FY2026, +13%). The pure-play S2P suites are all now private. Coupa was the largest, at $725M in FY2022 and about $818M TTM when it was taken private. Jaggaer is about $300M. Ivalua, GEP and Zycus disclose nothing, and third-party estimates for them conflict. Among spend-management fintechs, BILL ($1.46B FY2025, +13%) and Navan ($702M FY2026, +31%) are public with modest-to-good growth. Ramp is the outlier, at more than $2B annualized revenue by its own claim (Oct 2026).

### Inferences
- The legacy S2P suites (Coupa, Jaggaer, Ivalua, GEP, Zycus) sit in a band of roughly $0.2–1B in revenue, growing about 10–25%. Coupa is probably at or near $1B today, but it is private and that is not verified.

## Key Question 2: What EV/revenue multiples have procurement/spend-management assets fetched in M&A?

### Takeaway
Control deals for mature procurement and spend assets have priced at roughly **7–10x revenue**. Coupa went at about 9.8x trailing (8.4–8.5x NTM), Jaggaer at about 10x revenue / about 24x EBITDA (reported), and Brex at about 7x run-rate. Growth-equity marks for hyper-growth leaders run far higher: Ramp at about 30x claimed annualized revenue. Public spend-tech has re-rated lower. Navan IPO'd at about 8.8x FY revenue, below its 2022 private mark, and Brex sold about 58–60% below its peak valuation.

### Inferences

- Tuck-ins in the $300–550M range (Airbase, Scout RFP) show strategics will pay meaningful prices for spend and sourcing point solutions, though no revenue multiples were disclosed.

## Key Question 3: Which of these companies are plausible acquirers of a company like Vertice?

### Takeaway
The most plausible acquirers are (1) **Coupa / Thoma Bravo**, an active AI-intake and procurement buyer with four acquisitions since May 2025; (2) **SAP and Workday**, ERP/HCM suites that have bought sourcing, contract-AI and digital-adoption assets; (3) **ServiceNow and Flexera**, which own the IT/SAM buyer; and (4) **Ramp**, plus HCM/payroll players such as **Deel** and **Paylocity** that are buying their way into spend. PE platforms (Vista/Jaggaer, Thoma Bravo/Coupa, K1/Emburse) could pursue add-ons. BILL is more likely a target than a buyer, and Brex now sits inside Capital One.

### Inferences
- **Tier 1 (highest strategic fit):** Coupa (Thoma Bravo), because intake-to-procure plus SaaS/indirect pricing data complements its Tonkean intake and Scoutbee supplier-discovery deals. Ramp, because procurement and AI negotiation extend its spend platform and it has the currency ($60B valuation, fresh cash). Workday, as a sourcing-plus-contract-AI follow-on to Scout RFP and Evisort.
- **Tier 2:** SAP (Ariba modernization and pricing data), ServiceNow (ties SaaS procurement to its SAM/ITSM base; the $13.3B revenue base makes a deal immaterial in size), Flexera (SaaS spend plus SAM), Deel and Paylocity (HCM-led spend expansion), and Vista/Jaggaer or Ivalua (PE add-ons).
- **Less likely:** BILL, which is itself in play and is SMB AP-focused; Capital One/Brex, which is still integrating; Expensify, which is too small at $142M revenue with negative earnings.
- The Vendr data asset (more than $75B of spend benchmarks) probably makes Vertice more attractive to data- and AI-focused acquirers. This is inference, not reported.
